The tally on the clay

A clay tablet from an annealing shed. The batch number is not written in digits. You reply, and the environment scores you.

A script used in one shed

The night shift at one glassworks marks annealing batches on clay. The tally is used inside that shed and nowhere else. A notch cut into the top of the tablet is five. A pin in the lower field is one. That sum is the raw tally. A copper line across the middle means the tablet was turned on the fork, and the batch number is nine minus the raw tally. Specks along the sides are ash. They are not pins.

Nothing in the picture is a decimal digit. The prompt does not contain the number either. A finished batch episode is one digit, from 0 to 9.

What an environment is

The model does not own the shed. It cannot pick the tablet, and it must not be handed the reading. Another program does that work. It chooses the clay, draws the picture, writes the question, and scores the reply. That program is the environment.

An episode is three calls. reset starts it and returns an observation: the picture and the prompt. The action is the reply, one token. step sends that reply back and returns a reward. Then the episode is over. The card above is that loop. Reset, a guess, a score. The reading appears only with the score.

The environment is there because the tally is not a sentence the model already knows. At the start the weights are random. A convolution can see pixels, and a row of characters can see the question, but nothing in those weights is a notch or a pin. The only teacher is the number that comes back after the reply.

Three questions, one piece of clay

Batch is one step. Reply with the batch number, turn line included. An exact digit scores 1. A neighbor scores 0.2. Anything else scores 0. There is one correct digit, so the reward points at it.

Read this annealing tablet.
Reply with the batch number.

Pins is the same tablet and a different sentence. Reply with how many pins are in the lower field. Notches and the copper line do not count. The picture can stay still while the words change the answer. That is why the prompt is part of the observation, not a caption glued to the image.

Fork puts two tablets on the annealer’s fork. The lower batch leaves the kiln first. Reply left or right. A walk from one side to the other is not required. The score is 1 when the side is the lower batch, and 0 otherwise. That one is for a person. The policy was trained on a single tablet.

Why the update is a reward

The policy is a small vision-language model. A short convolution reads the tablet. The prompt is summed in as characters. The reply is one token, a digit.

Each training step lays out every legal reply for the same tablet. The environment scores them. The update raises the probability of a reply in proportion to the reward it just received. A group of identical scores would teach nothing, because nothing in the group is better. Here the scores differ: one digit is exact, a neighbor is close, and the rest are zero. The weights move toward the higher scores. They never receive the digit as a sentence to copy.

Reinforcement learning fits this shed because the label is a score, not a paragraph. Batch has one right digit, so that score is enough to climb. Pins is the same kind of score on a different question. Fork is a comparison. OpenEnv is the same reset and step behind a server, so a trainer can run the episode without holding the clay renderer.
